# Multi-agent LLM trading systems and the "jury of models" design — research brief

Date: 2026-09-07. Scope: what has been built, what the evidence says, how to design a debating jury of models that learns from its own track record. All claims are cited; where sources disagree or I could not reach a primary source, it is flagged.

## TL;DR

- Every published multi-agent trading paper reports strong backtests; every live or leakage-controlled benchmark in 2025–2026 finds little or no persistent alpha. Treat published Sharpe ratios as upper bounds on hype, not on skill.
- The dominant failure modes in live LLM trading (Alpha Arena, LiveTradeBench, StockBench) are behavioural, not analytical: overtrading, leverage, long bias, and prompt-locked "personalities". Risk plumbing matters more than the model.
- Debate helps when roles are adversarial *and* aggregation is deterministic. A single LLM judge is the weakest link (family bias, persuadability by confident-but-wrong agents). Use a cross-family jury with confidence-weighted or conviction-weighted voting, and let a judge only resolve ties or veto.
- Diminishing returns arrive fast: 3–5 agents and 2 rounds capture nearly all the gain; token cost grows 17–49x beyond that.
- Contamination is the central validity threat. Backtests overlapping a model's training window can be inflated by 45%+ (single-stock returns) and the only clean protocol is forward-only: timestamped live inputs, forecasts recorded before outcomes, scored later.
- You need roughly 100 trades to detect a 60% hit rate, roughly 400–800 to confirm a 55% one, and 1–3 years of daily returns to confirm a Sharpe of 1. Design the learning loop for that horizon.

## 1. Existing systems

### 1.1 Trading-firm-shaped multi-agent frameworks

**TradingAgents (Tauric Research, Dec 2024).** Pipeline: an analyst team (fundamentals, sentiment, news, technicals) feeds bull and bear researchers who hold multi-round debates; a trader synthesises; a risk team argues from aggressive/neutral/conservative stances; a fund manager approves. All agents use ReAct; "quick-thinking" gpt-4o/gpt-4o-mini handle summarisation while o1-preview does reasoning. Backtest: AAPL, GOOGL, AMZN, Jan 1–Mar 29 2024 vs buy-and-hold, MACD, KDJ+RSI, ZMR, SMA. Reported: AAPL +26.6% cumulative (Sharpe 8.21), GOOGL +24.4%, AMZN +23.2%, max drawdown 0.9–2.1%. The authors themselves flag the 3-month window ("11 LLM calls & 20+ tool calls/prediction") and attribute the extreme Sharpe to a period with few pullbacks ([arXiv 2412.20138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20138), [GitHub](https://github.com/tauricresearch/tradingagents)). Weaknesses: three stocks, one quarter, one bull regime, LLM nondeterminism, and no live results. A 2026 reproducibility study exists ([ACM AI & Fintech 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3800973.3801029), paywalled — could not verify its conclusions). A separate 2026 audit of 30 LLM-trading papers found only 14 report transaction-cost treatment and that a 10 bp cost cuts an LLM-style daily strategy's return by ~11% relative, 25 bp by ~26% ([arXiv 2606.08285](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08285)). One positive: gpt-4o and o1-preview have October 2023 cutoffs, so the Q1 2024 window is post-cutoff ([knowledge-cutoff list](https://otterly.ai/blog/knowledge-cutoff/)).

**FinMem (Nov 2023, AAAI-SS / IEEE).** Single agent with (a) a profile incl. risk inclination, (b) three-layer memory — shallow (daily news, 14-day decay), intermediate (10-Q, 90-day), deep (10-K, 365-day) — each event scored by recency + relevancy + importance, (c) immediate reflection (daily decision) and extended reflection (rolling M-day P&L review written back into deep memory), and (d) a self-adaptive risk switch that turns conservative when 3-day cumulative return is negative. Test Oct 2022–Apr 2023 on TSLA/NFLX/AMZN/MSFT/COIN with GPT-4-Turbo best; e.g. TSLA +61.8%, Sharpe 2.68 vs buy-and-hold −18.6% ([arXiv 2311.13743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13743), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.13743), [GitHub](https://github.com/pipiku915/FinMem-LLM-StockTrading)). Weakness: GPT-4-Turbo's training cutoff (April 2023, later December 2023) *contains* the test window, so parametric look-ahead cannot be excluded ([cutoff list](https://otterly.ai/blog/knowledge-cutoff/)).

**FinAgent (KDD 2024).** Multimodal market-intelligence module (numbers, text, charts), tool augmentation with expert strategies, diversified memory retrieval, and *dual-level reflection* (low-level: reflect on price moves; high-level: reflect on trade decisions). Claims >36% average profit improvement over 9 baselines across 6 stock/crypto datasets ([arXiv 2402.18485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18485), [KDD](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3637528.3671801)). The abstract does not discuss contamination.

**FinRobot (AI4Finance, May 2024).** An open-source *platform* (agent layer with "Financial Chain-of-Thought", LLM-algorithm layer, LLMOps/DataOps, multi-source model layer), not a trading-results paper ([arXiv 2405.14767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14767)).

**StockAgent (Jul 2024, ACM TIST).** A market *simulator* populated by LLM investors, built to avoid test-set leakage. Findings worth stealing: GPT agents traded less often but in far larger size (384 vs 800 trades; 176.7M vs 3.6M shares) and were more optimistic and more individually diverse; Gemini agents were more pessimistic, short-leaning and herd-like; removing interest-rate information increased trading; removing earnings reports flipped agents from loss to profit; backbone choice dominates outcomes ([arXiv 2407.18957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18957), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18957v5)).

**Alpha-GPT / Alpha-GPT 2.0 (2023–2024).** LLM as interactive *factor* miner, not a trader; reported top-10 of 41,000 teams in WorldQuant's IQC ([arXiv 2308.00016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00016), [2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09746)).

**AlphaAgents (BlackRock authors, Aug 2025).** Fundamental, sentiment and valuation agents in an AutoGen group chat; a coordinator ensures each speaks at least twice; round-robin until consensus; GPT-4o. 15 tech stocks, Feb–May 2024. Risk-neutral multi-agent beat single-agent and benchmark; risk-averse variants underperformed in the rally; a "risk-seeking" prompt was indistinguishable from risk-neutral — i.e. persona prompts have limited steering power ([arXiv 2508.11152](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11152)).

**QuantAgents (EMNLP 2025).** Four agents (simulated-trading, risk, news, manager) that meet; dual feedback from real-market P&L and simulated-trading forecast accuracy; claims ~300% over three years, retrospectively ([arXiv 2510.04643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04643)).

**virattt/ai-hedge-fund (63k GitHub stars, active Sept 2026).** Educational-only disclaimer. Being rebuilt as pluggable "alpha models": LLM investor personas (Buffett, Munger, Graham, Lynch, Druckenmiller) plus quant models (PEAD). Each LLM agent must return `{signal: bullish|neutral|bearish, confidence: 0–100, reasoning}`; value = sign x confidence/100; LLM/parse failures *abstain* with an `abstained` flag; the portfolio constructor is pure arithmetic — a conviction-weighted mean over voting models with abstentions excluded from numerator and denominator ("no opinion must not masquerade as opinion: neutral"), then risk limits clamp sizes ("conviction requests, risk disposes"); every prompt+response is cached and persisted ([repo](https://github.com/virattt/ai-hedge-fund), [llm_agent.py](https://github.com/virattt/ai-hedge-fund/blob/main/hedge_fund/signals/llm_agent.py), [construction.py](https://github.com/virattt/ai-hedge-fund/blob/main/hedge_fund/portfolio/construction.py)). This is the cleanest open-source template for a deterministic jury.

### 1.2 The debate papers the trading systems borrow from

- **Du et al. 2023 (ICML 2024).** Multiple instances answer, read each other's answers, revise; default 3 agents x 2 rounds. Accuracy rises with agents and rounds, but rounds beyond 4 add nothing; mixing models helped (on 20 GSM8K items Bard solved 11, ChatGPT 14, jointly 17). Known failure: agents "confidently affirm" a shared wrong answer ([arXiv 2305.14325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325)).
- **Liang et al. 2023 (EMNLP 2024).** Names the *Degeneration-of-Thought* problem (a model that has committed cannot self-correct by reflection) and fixes it with affirmative/negative debaters and a judge with adaptive stopping. Key warnings: only a "modest" level of tit-for-tat works, and "LLMs might not be a fair judge if different LLMs are used for agents" ([arXiv 2305.19118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19118)).

### 1.3 2025–2026 benchmarks and audits (the sobering part)

- **LiveTradeBench (UIUC, Nov 2025):** 21 LLMs, 50 live days (Aug 18–Oct 24 2025), 15 US equities/ETFs + 10 Polymarket contracts. Best stock Sharpe: GPT-4.1 (2.64, +6.25%), o3 (2.57), Qwen2.5-72B (2.18). Spearman correlation between LMArena score and stock return: 0.054. Cross-market Sharpe correlation ~0. Reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-thinking, o3) were the most volatile on Polymarket — "over-adjustment". Models have stable style signatures (Claude Opus 4.1 and Grok 4 conservative; Kimi K2 and GPT-5 aggressive) ([arXiv 2511.03628](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1)).
- **Agent Market Arena (WWW 2026):** four agent frameworks x five backbones, live crypto and stocks: "agent frameworks display markedly distinct behavioral patterns... whereas model backbones contribute less to outcome variation" ([arXiv 2510.11695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11695), [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3774904.3792821)).
- **StockBench (Oct 2025):** contamination-free Mar–Jul 2025 window; most agents fail to beat buy-and-hold; they beat it in the May–Aug 2025 upturn and lose in the Jan–Apr 2025 downturn — a structural long bias ([arXiv 2510.02209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02209)).
- **KTD-Fin (May 2026):** masks tickers and calendar dates so the model cannot recall history; ten frontier agents on CSI300 2024–2026; cumulative returns "largely explained by passive market and style exposure, with limited evidence of persistent stock-selection alpha" ([arXiv 2605.28359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28359)).
- **Coordination primacy (Mar 2026):** how agents coordinate matters more than how many; gains plateau; cost must be in the objective ([arXiv 2603.27539](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.27539)).
- **Agentic Trading audit (May 2026):** of 77 studies, 19 empirical; only 2/19 give time-consistent splits, 1/19 handles survivorship, 0/19 fully reproducible ([arXiv 2605.19337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19337)). See also the cost-aware closed-loop benchmark CLQT ([arXiv 2606.29771](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29771)).

### 1.4 Alpha Arena (nof1.ai): frontier models trading real money

**Season 1 (Oct 18–Nov 3 2025).** Six models — Qwen3 Max, DeepSeek V3.1, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Grok 4, GPT-5 — each given $10,000 of real USDC on Hyperliquid perpetuals (BTC/ETH/SOL/BNB/DOGE/XRP), identical prompts and data, reasoning logs public. Final: Qwen3 Max +22.3% (~$12.2k), DeepSeek +4.9%; the four Western models all lost, GPT-5 worst at about −63% (~$3.7k–4.1k). Sources differ on the losers' exact balances (ForkLog: Claude $5,799, Gemini $5,445, Grok $4,208, GPT-5 $4,126; other outlets quote Claude −31%, Grok −45%, Gemini −57%) ([iWeaver S1 results](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trading-season-1-results/), [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/), [GNcrypto](https://www.gncrypto.news/news/qwen-wins-alpha-arena-season-1-with-22-percent-returns/), [nof1.ai](https://nof1.ai/)).

Mid-competition press snapshots (secondary sources; nof1's own site blocked fetches): Gemini ran ~165 trades, paid >$1,000 in fees, ~25% win rate, ~14x leverage; GPT-5 ~17x leverage and repeated margin calls; Qwen ~43 trades (<3/day) ([iWeaver showdown](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trader-showdown/), [Medium](https://medium.com/@farhat.hanne/alpha-arena-when-ais-fight-who-will-survive-3b179c84f7fa), [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ai-crypto-trading-showdown-deepseek-202512124.html)). nof1 founder Jay Azhang: models "showed consistent biases that persisted over time and across many prompt iterations" — an investing "personality" — and LLMs "do not handle numerical time series data well" ([GNcrypto](https://www.gncrypto.news/news/qwen-wins-alpha-arena-season-1-with-22-percent-returns/), [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)). Not disclosed: drawdown, Sharpe, funding costs — so the leaderboard is a single unreplicated run ([iWeaver](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trading-season-1-results/)).

**Season 1.5 (ended Dec 3 2025).** Multiple prompt variants run in parallel ("New Baseline", "Monk Mode", "Situational Awareness", "Max Leverage" up to 20x) across Gemini 3 Pro, Qwen3 Max, GPT-5.1, Grok 4, DeepSeek V3.1, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Kimi K2 Thinking and a "Mystery Model" (reported to be a Grok 4.20 build). Only the Mystery Model was profitable across all four sub-competitions (+10–12%); DeepSeek 2nd, GPT-5.1 3rd; press reported 32 instances managing $320k ([OneDayAdvisor S1.5 review](https://www.onedayadvisor.com/2025/12/nof1ai-alpha-arena-review-season-15.html), [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/alpha-arena-nof1-ai-explained)). A Season 2 with a human trader was announced ([Phemex](https://phemex.com/news/article/nof1s-alpha-arena-tests-ai-models-in-live-trading-with-sui-group-and-karatage-support-81921)); I found no published Season 2 results as of Sept 2026.

**Lessons.** (1) Overtrading and fees killed Gemini; leverage killed GPT-5 — risk plumbing beat "intelligence". (2) Same prompt, wildly different behaviour: prompt-induced personality is stable and must be measured, not assumed. (3) Numeric time series in the prompt are handled badly; give models features and narrative, not raw candles. (4) One 17-day run proves nothing; nof1 itself moved to parallel prompt variants to get variance estimates.

## 2. What the literature says about LLMs forecasting markets

**The positive result.** Lopez-Lira & Tang asked ChatGPT whether a headline was good or bad for a stock, using only post-cutoff headlines (Oct 2021 onward). GPT-4 reached ~90% portfolio-day hit rates on the (non-tradable) initial reaction and significantly predicted subsequent drift, especially for small caps and negative news; predictability increases with model size and *decays as LLM adoption rises* ([arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619), [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4412788)). Read carefully: it is a news-sentiment result, sample restricted to post-cutoff, and the edge is decaying.

**The contamination problem.** 
- Glasserman & Lin: two biases — *look-ahead* (the model knows what followed the article) and *distraction* (general knowledge about the named firm contaminates sentiment). Anonymising company names removed both and the anonymised headlines actually performed better ([arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17322)).
- Sarkar & Vafa (ICML 2025): "Lookahead Propensity" — estimate whether the prompt text was in pretraining; a positive correlation between LAP and forecast accuracy proves look-ahead. Found in earnings-call risk prediction and election forecasting. "Prompting-based approaches do not eliminate the potential for lookahead bias"; only models trained on pre-period data do ([SSRN 4754678](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4754678), [ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/51018)).
- Gao, Jiang & Yan (Dec 2025): a direct test comparing forecasts with and without post-date information; substantial bias, worst for financial tasks ([arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.23847)).
- MemGuard-Alpha (Mar 2026): as contamination rises, in-sample accuracy rises 40.8% to 52.5% while out-of-sample accuracy *falls* 47% to 42%; clean signals earn 14.5 bp/day vs 2.1 bp for tainted ones; cross-model disagreement (different cutoffs) separates memory from reasoning ([arXiv 2603.26797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26797)).
- "Summoning the Oracle to Slay It" (May 2026): parametric look-ahead "lives inside the model's weights". Removing it (FinCAD) cut Qwen2.5-14B's 2010–2020 single-stock backtest returns by 45% on average (NVDA −78%, MSFT −61%) while leaving 2025 out-of-sample results unchanged ([arXiv 2605.24564](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24564)).
- Look-Ahead-Bench (Jan 2026) measures alpha decay across periods to separate memorisation from skill and finds substantial bias in standard LLMs ([arXiv 2601.13770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13770)). A 2025 *Economics Letters* note reports memorisation is strongest for indexes and low-frequency data and weakest for high-frequency single-stock data and smaller models (paywalled; [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165176525004392)).
- A 2026 review of 164 finance-LLM papers found no single bias (look-ahead, survivorship, narrative, objective, cost) is discussed in more than 28% of studies ([arXiv 2602.14233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14233)).

**Operating rules that follow.**
1. Forward-only evaluation. Never backtest a hosted model over dates before its cutoff. Every input carries a timestamp; every forecast is written to an append-only log *before* the outcome exists; scoring runs later. StockBench and LiveTradeBench are the reference designs.
2. Ask "what will happen given these facts", never "what happened". Do not put dates, tickers or company names in the prompt if you can avoid it (Glasserman-Lin anonymisation; KTD-Fin masking). Where you must name the ticker, at least strip the calendar date and give relative times ("3 days ago").
3. Feed same-day live data only: a numbered list of headlines with publication timestamps, point-in-time fundamentals, derived features. Require agents to cite evidence by index so you can audit for hallucinated facts.
4. Use cross-model disagreement as a contamination alarm: if models with different cutoffs agree on a prediction only when the event is old, suspect memory (MemGuard-Alpha).
5. Keep a canary: periodically ask each model to forecast a *past* period it cannot know and one it can; a gap reveals memorisation (Sarkar-Vafa logic).
6. Model the frictions. Report every result net of realistic costs and with explicit execution timing (signal after close, fill next open/close) ([arXiv 2606.08285](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08285)).

## 3. Debate / jury design patterns that empirically help

**Role diversity, and why it is not enough by itself.** Bull/bear pairs (TradingAgents), affirmative/negative + judge (Liang), and risk personas all exist to fight Degeneration-of-Thought. But a 2025 analysis of *why* debate fails shows competitive debate degenerates into "cheap talk" — debaters "present claims in an overconfident tone to mislead the judge" — while consensus-seeking debate suppresses informative disagreement so strong agents defer to weak ones. Their fix, Collaborative MAD, adds quote-based evidence verification, self-audit of failure modes and calibrated confidence for the judge, and gains 4–10 points at equal token budget. They also show cross-family pairs (e.g. GPT-4 + Llama-2) could remove >30% of errors because error patterns are complementary ([arXiv 2510.20963](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20963)). A 2026 survey confirms 81.5% of debate systems use one model family and names the three recurring failures: inter-agent sycophancy/conformity, *debate collapse* (premature shared error), and judge bias ([arXiv 2607.26212](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26212v1); collapse metrics in [arXiv 2602.07186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07186)). Practical rule: a jury must span at least three model families, and each seat gets a *different* information diet (technicals only, news only, fundamentals only) so disagreements are real rather than stylistic.

**Structured rounds.** Opening (independent, blind to peers) -> rebuttal (each agent sees the others' JSON, must address the strongest counter-argument by index) -> final vote (revised JSON). Du et al. found gains saturate by ~4 rounds; GroupDebate measured token cost rising 36–49x going from 1 to 8 agents at 4 rounds and 17–29x going from 1 to 4 rounds at 4 agents ([arXiv 2409.14051](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.14051)); studies in this literature report accuracy saturating around 5 agents (e.g. 72% to 90% going from 1 to 5 agents on arithmetic, a plateau at 5–7 agents on MMLU) and degrading past ~4 rounds ([GroupDebate](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14051), [survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26212v1)). Smit et al. (ICML 2024) found untuned debate does not reliably beat self-consistency or ensembling ([arXiv 2311.17371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17371)); Google showed sparse communication (each agent sees a subset of peers) matches fully-connected accuracy at much lower cost ([EMNLP 2024 Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.427/)). Budget rule: **3–5 agents, 2 rounds** (opening + one rebuttal), sparse exposure in the rebuttal (each agent sees only the two most-opposed peers), and a *deterministic* aggregation step instead of a third round.

**Force numbers and invalidation.** Require `confidence` in [0,1], an explicit `what_would_prove_me_wrong`, and a stop level. Verbalised confidence from RLHF models is better calibrated than token probabilities (ECE cut ~50% relative) but systematically overconfident in the 0.7–1.0 band ([Tian et al., EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.330/)); confident-but-wrong agents reliably beat truthful-but-uncertain ones in front of LLM judges ("The Confident Liar", [arXiv 2606.10296](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10296)). So the confidence field is an *input to scoring*, not a persuasion device: show judges each model's rolling Brier score alongside its stated confidence, and shrink stated confidence toward each model's empirical calibration curve before voting.

**Abstain as a first-class outcome.** The ai-hedge-fund blend excludes abstentions from both numerator and denominator so "no opinion" never becomes "neutral" ([construction.py](https://github.com/virattt/ai-hedge-fund/blob/main/hedge_fund/portfolio/construction.py)). Reasoning models are notably bad at recognising unanswerable questions ([AbstentionBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09038); survey [arXiv 2407.18418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18418)), so make `direction: "none"` a schema value, reward it in scoring when the realised move stays inside the noise band, and require a minimum number of non-abstaining voters before any trade.

**Aggregation: vote, don't adjudicate.** ReConcile's confidence-weighted vote across ChatGPT/Bard/Claude beat single- and multi-agent baselines by 7.7% ([arXiv 2309.13007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13007)); a Panel of LLM evaluators from disjoint families beat a single GPT-4 judge with less intra-model bias at ~1/7 the cost ([PoLL, arXiv 2404.18796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796)); majority voting is competitive on knowledge-type questions while consensus helps only on multi-step reasoning ([arXiv 2502.19130](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.19130)). Why a single judge dominates: judges prefer outputs from their own family and lower-perplexity text ([self-preference bias, arXiv 2410.21819](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21819); [family bias, arXiv 2508.06709](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.06709)), exhibit position bias, and debate *amplifies* judge bias after round one ([Adaline summary](https://www.adaline.ai/blog/llm-as-a-judge-reliability-bias)); Liang et al. warned the same. Debate does make judges more accurate — 76% (LLM) / 88% (human) vs 48% / 60% naive — but only under adversarial pressure; non-adversarial "consultancy" makes persuasive wrong answers *more* convincing ([Khan et al., ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06782)).

Recommended aggregation: position-weighted vote, `score_t = sum_m w_m * dir_m * conf_m_calibrated * size_m`, with `w_m` from the model's leaderboard (section 4), abstentions excluded, a minimum quorum, and a trade only when |score| clears a threshold *and* the bear side's best invalidation is not already triggered. If you keep a judge, rotate it across families each session, hide agent identities (anonymised debate reduces identity-driven bias, [arXiv 2510.07517](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.07517)), and give it veto power only (it may downgrade to no-trade; it may not originate a trade).

**Risk manager as code, not prose.** Alpha Arena's losers were killed by leverage and fees, not bad theses. Position size, max leverage, stop distance and daily trade count are deterministic constraints applied *after* the vote — "conviction requests, risk disposes".

## 4. Self-improvement loop

**Storing post-mortems.** FinMem writes rolling P&L reflections into deep memory; FinAgent reflects at two levels (price moves, then decisions); Reflexion keeps verbal self-critiques in an episodic buffer that is prepended to later prompts ([NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/1b44b878bb782e6954cd888628510e90-Paper-Conference.pdf)); ExpeL extracts cross-episode *insights* from an experience pool ([AAAI 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10144)); Voyager stores verified reusable skills ([survey](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0020174X.2024.2439995)); Generative Agents retrieve by recency x importance x relevance ([memory pattern](https://agentpatterns.ai/agent-design/generative-agents-memory-stream/)).

The most useful recent template is **ACE — Agentic Context Engineering (ICLR 2026)**: the "playbook" is a list of itemised bullets, each with an id and *helpful/harmful counters*; the generator flags which bullets it used, a reflector attributes success/failure to bullets, a curator applies small delta updates and de-duplicates by embedding. It exists specifically to avoid *context collapse* (a monolithic rewrite shrank a playbook from 18,282 to 122 tokens and dropped accuracy 66.7% to 57.1%) and *brevity bias*; it gained +17.1% on AppWorld and +7.6% on the FiNER finance task ([arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618v1), [ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/8a94ff6f922d995d7d3f4ebf4143e442-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

**Keeping lessons small and high-signal.** Adapt ACE's counters to P&L: for each lesson store `{id, text, scope (ticker/sector/regime/instrument), created_from (trade ids), applied_count, counterfactual_R}`. `counterfactual_R` is computed offline: for every later trade where the lesson was applicable, estimate the R-multiple with and without following it (e.g. "do not chase gaps >5%" -> would skipping have avoided a −1R?). Rank lessons by mean counterfactual R x sqrt(applied_count); inject only the top ~10 applicable to the current ticker/regime; decay unused lessons; merge near-duplicates; archive anything with negative counterfactual R after 10+ applications. Write lessons as *conditions plus actions* ("when X and Y, do Z") with no dates or tickers embedded, so they transfer and do not leak history back into prompts.

**Per-model shadow portfolios and a leaderboard.** Execute the consensus, but *record* every model's own proposal and mark it to market as a paper "shadow portfolio" with the same costs and sizing rules — LiveTradeBench and Agent Market Arena do exactly this per model ([2511.03628](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1), [2510.11695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11695)). Rate models with **Bradley-Terry** on pairwise trade outcomes (LMArena moved from Elo to BT for order-invariance and stability; [LMSYS](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2023-12-07-leaderboard/), [comparison](https://hippocampus-garden.com/elo_vs_bt/)) or **TrueSkill**, which carries a per-model uncertainty (mu, sigma) and copes with sparse data and >2 players ([Herbrich et al., NIPS 2006](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/3079-trueskilltm-a-bayesian-skill-rating-system)). Each session is a "match": every model's realised R on that ticker, ties for abstentions. Convert rating to vote weight conservatively (mu − k·sigma) so a lucky newcomer cannot dominate. Compare each model's shadow Sharpe against the consensus portfolio; if a single model beats the jury persistently over ~1 year, the jury is destroying information (re-weight). Tetlock's tournament evidence supports weighting by track record — forecaster skill correlated 0.65 year to year — while "extremizing" the aggregate may have been a fluke ([AI Impacts](https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/)).

**Detecting confidently-wrong models.** Bucket each model's stated confidence (0.5–0.6, ..., 0.9–1.0), plot realised hit rate per bucket (reliability diagram), and decompose the Brier score into reliability, resolution, uncertainty (Murphy 1973; [decomposition note](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/articles/journal_contribution/Simplifying_and_generalising_Murphy_s_Brier_score_decomposition/29748851/1/files/56771708.pdf); ECE per [Guo et al. 2017 summary](https://metricgate.com/blogs/brier-score-vs-log-loss-vs-calibration/)). A model whose 0.9 bucket hits 55% is confidently wrong: remap its stated confidence through its own isotonic/Platt curve before voting, and demote its vote weight if reliability stays poor after ~50 forecasts. LLM overconfidence is structural (RLHF rewards decisive tone; [arXiv 2606.03437](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03437)), so expect to recalibrate every model.

## 5. Practical prompt and schema design

**Prompt skeleton (per agent).** System: role and information diet, the ten highest-ranked applicable lessons, the model's own calibration note ("your 0.8+ calls hit 58%"). User: `as_of` (relative time only), instrument facts, numbered evidence list with timestamps, current position, risk limits, and the instruction: "Forecast what will happen over the horizon given only the evidence below. Cite evidence by index. If the evidence does not justify a position, return direction none."

**Trade proposal schema (JSON Schema, strict).**

```json
{
  "type": "object", "additionalProperties": false,
  "required": ["ticker","direction","instrument","entry_type","entry_price_ref","stop","target",
               "horizon_days","size_pct_of_equity","confidence","thesis","catalyst",
               "what_would_prove_me_wrong","key_risks","evidence_indices"],
  "properties": {
    "ticker": {"type":"string"},
    "direction": {"enum":["long","short","none"]},
    "instrument": {"enum":["shares","call","put","call_spread","put_spread","perp","none"]},
    "entry_type": {"enum":["market","limit","stop_entry","none"]},
    "entry_price_ref": {"type":"number","description":"reference price at as_of"},
    "stop": {"type":"number"},
    "target": {"type":"number"},
    "horizon_days": {"type":"integer","minimum":1,"maximum":60},
    "size_pct_of_equity": {"type":"number","minimum":0,"maximum":5},
    "confidence": {"type":"number","minimum":0,"maximum":1,
                   "description":"P(target hit before stop) within horizon"},
    "thesis": {"type":"string","maxLength":600},
    "catalyst": {"type":"string","maxLength":200},
    "what_would_prove_me_wrong": {"type":"string","maxLength":300},
    "key_risks": {"type":"array","items":{"type":"string"},"maxItems":5},
    "evidence_indices": {"type":"array","items":{"type":"integer"},"maxItems":10}
  }
}
```

Make `direction: "none"` require `size_pct_of_equity: 0`; validate in code that `stop` is on the correct side of `entry_price_ref`, that reward:risk >= 1, and that every `evidence_indices` entry exists — reject and re-prompt otherwise.

**Judge / aggregation verdict schema.**

```json
{
  "type":"object","additionalProperties":false,
  "required":["decision","weighted_score","quorum_met","votes","invalidations_triggered",
              "risk_adjusted_size_pct","dissent_summary","abstain_reason"],
  "properties":{
    "decision":{"enum":["long","short","no_trade"]},
    "weighted_score":{"type":"number"},
    "quorum_met":{"type":"boolean"},
    "votes":{"type":"array","items":{"type":"object","properties":{
       "model":{"type":"string"},"direction":{"enum":["long","short","none"]},
       "confidence_stated":{"type":"number"},"confidence_calibrated":{"type":"number"},
       "weight":{"type":"number"}},"required":["model","direction","confidence_stated","confidence_calibrated","weight"]}},
    "invalidations_triggered":{"type":"array","items":{"type":"string"}},
    "risk_adjusted_size_pct":{"type":"number"},
    "dissent_summary":{"type":"string","maxLength":400},
    "abstain_reason":{"type":["string","null"]}
  }
}
```

The numeric fields are filled by code; if an LLM judge is used it only writes `dissent_summary` and may flip `decision` to `no_trade`.

**Structured outputs on OpenRouter.** Send `response_format: {type:"json_schema", json_schema:{name, strict:true, schema}}`; support is per *endpoint*, so set `provider: {require_parameters: true}` to route only to providers that honour the schema, and filter models with `GET /api/v1/models?supported_parameters=structured_outputs`. OpenRouter lists OpenAI, Google Gemini, Anthropic and Fireworks as enforcing families and warns some providers "treat it as a strong hint" ([OpenRouter docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/structured-outputs)). Direct APIs: OpenAI strict mode is guaranteed; Anthropic structured outputs are GA for the Claude 4.5 family via `output_config.format` ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)); xAI Grok supports strict schemas ([xAI docs](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/text/structured-outputs)); DeepSeek's first-party API offers only `json_object` (json_schema rejected), though V4 gets schema enforcement through hosts like DeepInfra ([DeepSeek docs](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/guides/json_mode/), [DeepInfra](https://docs.deepinfra.com/chat/structured-outputs)). Whatever the provider says, validate with Pydantic, log the raw text, and treat a parse failure as an abstention (the ai-hedge-fund contract).

## 6. Evaluation metrics

Track everything twice: per model (shadow portfolio) and for the consensus.

| Metric | Definition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hit rate | share of trades with realised R > 0 | needs the CI below |
| Avg R-multiple / expectancy | mean of (P&L / initial risk); expectancy = p·avgWinR − (1−p)·avgLossR | Van Tharp convention; +0.3–0.5R/trade is strong for active systems, <0.2R is eaten by costs ([P&L Ledger](https://www.pnlledger.com/expectancy-r-multiples-the-plain-english-guide/), [Van Tharp](https://vantharpinstitute.com/tharp-think-trading-concepts/)) |
| Profit factor | gross wins / gross losses | >1.5 healthy |
| Sharpe, max drawdown | on daily equity, net of costs | report probabilistic Sharpe too |
| Brier / log loss | on the directional call with stated confidence; Brier = mean (p − outcome)^2 | coin flip at p=0.5 scores 0.25; log-loss baseline ln 2 = 0.693 |
| Reliability / ECE | calibration gap by confidence bucket | per model, rolling |
| Edge over coin flip | hit rate − 0.5 with Wilson CI | see sample sizes |
| Edge over SPY | consensus return vs SPY buy-and-hold, same period, same costs | 79% of active large-cap funds lagged the S&P 500 in 2025 ([SPIVA](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-us-mid-year-2025)) — the honest bar |

**How many trades before you can conclude anything.** With a one-proportion z-test (alpha 0.05, 80% power; formula per [PSU STAT 509](https://online.stat.psu.edu/stat509/book/export/html/660), [binomial test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_test)): detecting 60% vs 50% needs ~150 trades (one-sided) to ~195 (two-sided); 55% vs 50% needs ~615–780; 65% vs 50% about 65–85. Put differently, with 100 trades you can only "see" a hit rate of about 60% or better; 55/100 is indistinguishable from luck (z = 1.0). For Sharpe, Bailey & Lopez de Prado's Minimum Track Record Length gives, under near-normal daily returns, roughly 680 daily observations (~2.7 years) to be 95% confident an annualised Sharpe of 1.0 exceeds zero, and ~170 days (~8 months) for a Sharpe of 2.0; skew and fat tails lengthen it ([MinTRL / PSR](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1821643), [Deflated Sharpe](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2460551)). Since you will also test several models and prompt variants, apply the Deflated Sharpe correction for multiple trials before believing a leaderboard winner. Log a first-look review at 100 consensus trades, a re-weighting review at ~400, and no claim of edge before that.
