# How Traders Actually Trade the News — and What the Evidence Says Works

Research brief, 2026-09-07. Evidence labels: **A** = peer-reviewed academic, replicated; **B** = academic but contested, decayed, or single-study; **P** = practitioner convention with some quantitative support; **F** = folklore (no rigorous evidence either way).

**Bottom line.** The reaction to the *number itself* is priced by machines within milliseconds, so retail cannot trade it. What retail *can* plausibly trade: (a) multi-day drift after quantitative fundamental news (earnings/guidance, policy surprises), (b) fading over-reactions to qualitative or transient shocks (geopolitics, repairable supply disruptions, "story" news), (c) trend/volatility regimes with strictly defined risk. Base rates are brutal (roughly 1–3% of persistent day traders are durably profitable), so the system should start as a paper-trading, expectancy-tracking environment.

## 1. Event taxonomy

| Event | Instruments most affected | Reaction window | Documented tendency after initial move |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CPI / PPI** | Treasuries (2y most), USD, equity index futures, gold | Jump within seconds; most price discovery in minutes; intraday reversals common | No reliable multi-day index drift documented; intraday reversals frequent (13 Oct 2022: S&P −2% to new lows, closed +2%) [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/news/22/10/29273266/historic-s-p-500-comeback-runs-out-of-steam-what-are-the-market-catalysts-ahead). In the 2021–25 inflation regime, downside CPI surprises moved the S&P far more than upside ones [Applied Economics Letters 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13504851.2026.2624038). **B** |
| **NFP / employment** | Treasuries, USD, index futures | Seconds to minutes | Employment, PPI, Fed-funds and CPI releases produce the sharpest Treasury moves; all 25 largest price changes in a year of Treasury data followed a release [Fleming & Remolona, NY Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/97v03n4/9712flem.html). Surprises (actual minus consensus) cause jumps; bad news moves more than good [Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold & Vega 2003](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/000282803321455151). No documented multi-day drift. **A** jump / **F** drift |
| **FOMC decision + statement + presser** | Everything; rates and USD first | Statement at 14:00, presser 14:30; sharp two-way moves | Two separable surprises, *target* rate and *path* (statement guidance), with different effects on yields and stocks [Gürkaynak, Sack & Swanson 2005](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ijc/ijcjou/y2005q2a2.html). **Drift**: returns continue in the surprise's direction; the easing-minus-tightening gap reaches >4.5% by day +15 ("monetary momentum") [Neuhierl & Weber, NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24748/w24748.pdf). **A/B** |
| **GDP, PMI, retail sales** | Same, smaller | Minutes | Lower tier in the Fleming-Remolona ranking; noise unless the surprise is large. **B** |
| **Central-bank speeches** | Rates, USD | Minutes to hours | Fed-chair speech tone tracks the fed-funds-futures slope, which predicts weekly equity returns (1994–2007) [Neuhierl & Weber 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304393219301400); since 1994 the equity premium is earned in *even* weeks of the FOMC cycle, plausibly via informal Fed communication [Cieslak, Morse & Vissing-Jorgensen 2019](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2687614). **B** |
| **Earnings + guidance** | Single stock, sector peers, options IV | Jump in after-hours/pre-market; most discovery immediate | **Drift** (PEAD): ~2% abnormal return over 60 days for good-news stocks; top-minus-bottom SUE decile positive in 41 of 48 quarters [Bernard & Thomas 1989](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v27y1989ip1-36.html); confirmed across many countries [PEAD review 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214635020303750). Guidance dominates: the penalty for downward guidance substantially exceeds the premium for beating [Accounting & Business Research 2011](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00014788.2011.550738). **A** |
| **M&A** | Target, acquirer, sector | Instant gap; spread persists to close | Target gaps to *below* the offer; spread of ~3–7% reflects completion risk; 70–90% of US deals close; arbs earned ~+2.0% on completed deals and −2.8% on failures (835 deals, 2000–2020) [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/merger-spread/), [AIMA](https://www.aima.org/asset/676DA5D6-8CE4-42D7-A2C6171EAC6382DC/). Acquirer typically falls. **A/P** |
| **Regulatory / legal rulings** | Single names, sector ETFs | Instant gap; permanence depends on whether the rule sticks | FDA: approvals ≈ +1.6% abnormal, rejections ≈ −21% (secondary source) [DrugPatentWatch](https://www.drugpatentwatch.com/blog/overnight-millionaires-or-a-decade-long-marathon-deconstructing-the-drug-patent-approval-effect-on-stocks/). Permanent cash-flow shocks never recover: TAL −70.8%, EDU −54.2% in one day on China's July 2021 tutoring ban [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/07/23/us-listed-china-education-stocks-plunge-as-beijing-regulators-crack-down.html). Reversible policy reverses: S&P −11% in two days after the April 2025 tariffs (energy −17%, staples −4%), then +9.5% in one day on the pause [SF Fed](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2025/10/market-reactions-to-tariff-announcements/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/04/09/stock-market-today-live-updates.html). **B** (cases) |
| **Geopolitical shocks** | Index, oil, gold, defense, USD/CHF | Hours to days | **Reversal**: across 20 post-WWII military events the S&P fell ~6% to trough on average and recovered in ~28 days in 19 of 20 [LPL](https://www.lpl.com/research/blog/middle-east-conflict-how-stocks-react-to-geopolitical-shock.html), [RBC](https://www.rbcwealthmanagement.com/en-us/insights/then-and-now-market-reactions-to-military-conflicts-and-what-they-mean-today). Commodity legs can persist when supply is genuinely impaired. **B** |
| **Commodity / energy supply events** | Crude, XLE/USO, airlines, refiners | Instant; fades if supply is restored | Abqaiq (Sept 2019): ~5% of world supply offline; crude +14% in a day, fully unwound by month-end once repairs were announced [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=41413), [CRS](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN11173/IN11173.2.pdf). OPEC cuts produce significant abnormal returns; "maintain" decisions do nothing [Energy Policy event study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301421515302007). **A/B**; reversal if supply is repaired, drift if not |
| **Crypto: ETF flows** | BTC, ETH, IBIT/FBTC, MSTR, COIN | Same day, with next-day predictability | $100M net inflow ≈ +53 bps same-day BTC return; flows explain 21% of daily variance and predict next-day returns, but individual flow shocks *reverse* once future flows are controlled for — the drift is a "flow-persistence illusion" [Lim, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6592830). Rolling 60-day flow/price correlation 0.10–0.50 [FalconX](https://www.falconx.io/newsroom/what-can-spot-etf-flows-tell-us-about-the-trajectory-of-bitcoin-prices-a-preliminary-statistical-investigation). **B** |
| **Crypto: exchange hacks / failures** | BTC, ETH, exchange tokens, COIN | Hours to a week | FTX (Nov 2022): BTC −22% in a week to ~$16.8k, recovered two months later [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/11/21/bitcoin-btc-ether-eth-fall-as-ftx-collapse-ripples-through-market.html), [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/cryptocurrencies/news/bitcoin-price-wipes-out-losses-from-ftx-exchange-collapse-hits-21-095-after-two-months-202301141331). Bybit ($1.5B, Feb 2025): BTC only ~2–4% down — contained [Alchemy Markets](https://alchemymarkets.com/education/market-insights/chart-of-the-day/bitcoin-hack-feb-2025/). Solvency events drift; pure theft with a solvent exchange reverses. **P** |
| **Crypto: regulation / protocol upgrades** | BTC, ETH | Days | Sell-the-news: BTC fell from the day after spot-ETF approval (10 Jan 2024), −15% by 13 Jan [itez](https://itez.com/en/blog/coins/spot-bitcoin-etfs-launch-in-the-us-takeaways); ETH −9% on Merge day, ~−20% over the following weeks [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2022/09/15/market-wrap-markets-trade-lower-despite-success-of-ethereum-merge), [CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/ethereum-merge-turns-into-sell-the-news-event-as-eth-price-plummets-20-since/). **P** (tiny n) |
| **Company idiosyncratic** | Single name | Instant gap | Launches and leadership changes ("qualitative story news") *over*react and give the move back over weeks; earnings/dividends/guidance *under*react and drift [Kargarzadeh et al. 2026, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14014). Recalls: −0.4% to −10.6% abnormal, worse for food, high-hazard and government-ordered [Marquette](https://epublications.marquette.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1332&context=mgmt_fac). CEO exits: forced ≈ +0.5%, scandal-driven negative, voluntary ≈ 0 [AAU](https://netlibrary.aau.at/obvuklhs/download/pdf/7152004), [Harvard Corp Gov](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2017/06/08/retired-or-fired-how-can-investors-tell-if-the-ceo-left-voluntarily/). **A/B** |

The organizing rule: **quantitative, cash-flow-relevant news under-reacts (drift); qualitative, transient, or anticipated news over-reacts (reversal).** In 4.57M articles across ~3,000 US stocks, the move accumulated by the close of publication day is ~2.8× the additional move over the next 20 days, and for rumor-flagged events the rumor day captures the entire move while confirmation adds nothing [arXiv 2608.14014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14014). Scheduled macro days also carry most of the equity premium: 11.4 bps average excess return on CPI/employment/FOMC days vs 1.1 bps on other days (1958–2009), over 60% of the annual premium [Savor & Wilson 2013](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1312091) — sitting flat through every announcement has a cost too.

## 2. Documented anomalies (and their decay)

- **PEAD** — Bernard & Thomas (1989): ~2% drift over 60 trading days; top-minus-bottom SUE decile ≈ 4% per quarter, positive in 41/48 quarters; interpreted as investors failing to see that earnings surprises persist [Bernard & Thomas](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v27y1989ip1-36.html). Still detectable internationally [review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214635020303750), though weaker in large liquid names. **A, decayed but alive.**
- **Pre-FOMC drift** — Lucca & Moench: +49 bps in the 24h before scheduled FOMC announcements (Sept 1994–Mar 2011), ~80% of annual equity excess returns [NBER conf. paper](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2013/MEs13/Lucca_Moench.pdf). Post-2011 it persisted only on press-conference meetings (~40 bps, front-loaded to the morning) [NY Fed 2018](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2018/11/the-pre-fomc-announcement-drift-more-recent-evidence/), and essentially disappeared after 2015 [Kurov et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7525326/). **B, largely dead.**
- **Post-FOMC drift ("monetary momentum")** — see Section 1; a 15-day hold in the surprise's direction had ~4× the Sharpe of buy-and-hold [Neuhierl & Weber](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24748/w24748.pdf). **B (no post-publication test).**
- **Buy the rumor, sell the news** — institutions buy ahead of analyst upgrades and sell on the announcement, but the same pattern was *not* found around earnings [Cornell/Irvine et al.](https://business.cornell.edu/news/2016/07/23/the-nuances-of-buy-the-rumor-sell-the-news/). The 2026 news-corpus study validates it strictly for rumor-flagged events [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14014). **B: true for anticipated qualitative events, false for earnings.**
- **Cross-sectional momentum** — Jegadeesh & Titman (1993): 3–12-month winners minus losers ≈ 1%/month (12/3 strategy 1.49%/month), partly dissipating in years 2–3 [JT 1993](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb04702.x). Risk: **momentum crashes** in panic states — Mar–May 2009 the loser decile rose 163% vs 8% for winners [Daniel & Moskowitz 2016](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439). **A, with fat left tail.**
- **Short-term reversal** — one-month losers beat winners by ~2%/month (1934–1987) [Jegadeesh 1990 via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/quantitative-momentum-research-short-term-return-reversal/); weekly losers rebound 0.9–1.2% [Lehmann 1990]. Much is bid-ask bounce, uncapturable after costs. **B.**
- **Under/over-reaction theory** — BSV: conservatism → underreaction, representativeness → overreaction to streaks [BSV 1998](https://www.nber.org/papers/w5926); DHS: overconfidence + self-attribution [DHS 1998]. The theoretical backbone for "drift, then reversal." **A (theory).**
- **News sentiment** — Tetlock (2007): WSJ-column pessimism predicts downward pressure *followed by reversion*; extreme pessimism predicts volume — sentiment is a noise-trader signal, not information [Tetlock 2007](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2007.01232.x). Loughran & McDonald (2011): ~3/4 of Harvard "negative" words are not negative in finance; their finance-specific lists relate to filing returns, volume and fraud [L&M 2011](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01625.x). **A.** Corollary: a market-level "negativity" score is a *fade* signal, not a follow signal.
- **Attention** — individuals are net buyers of attention-grabbing stocks (news, abnormal volume, extreme one-day moves); institutions are not [Barber & Odean 2008](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/21/2/785/1607197); Robinhood "Top Movers" herding is the modern version [Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.13183). **A.** Corollary: being on the top-movers list is a contrarian flag.
- **Surprise beats level** — every macro study above measures *actual minus consensus* (survey or fed-funds futures), not the raw number [Andersen et al.](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/000282803321455151), [GSS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ijc/ijcjou/y2005q2a2.html); PEAD sorts on SUE, not EPS. The *true* expectation can differ from published consensus: whisper numbers beat First Call in accuracy and as a proxy for market expectations [Bagnoli, Beneish & Watts 1999](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016541019900018X). Non-EPS surprises (revenue, margin, guidance) explain much of the announcement return [ABR 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00014788.2024.2400875). **A.**
- **Decay** — McLean & Pontiff (2016), 97 predictors: returns 26% lower out-of-sample, 58% lower post-publication; decay is largest for high in-sample returns, smallest in illiquid, high-idiosyncratic-vol stocks [McLean & Pontiff](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365). **A.** Assume half the paper value, with the residual in names too small or costly for institutions.

## 3. How a discretionary event-driven trader structures a trade

1. **Catalyst.** Scheduled (CPI, FOMC, earnings, PDUFA) or unscheduled (hack, ruling, attack). Scheduled events have a consensus and an options-implied move; unscheduled ones do not, which is where over-reaction is most likely.
2. **Expected vs. priced-in.** Three inputs: (a) consensus, its dispersion, and the whisper if available; (b) the **implied move** ≈ at-the-money straddle price ÷ spot for the expiry covering the event [MenthorQ](https://menthorq.com/guide/from-straddle-price-to-expected-move/); (c) positioning/sentiment (run-up, short interest, attention). Practitioner data say the implied move overestimates the realized move ~70% of the time (stocks stay inside it 70–75% vs 68% theoretical) [OptionsPilot](https://optionspilot.app/blog/options-implied-move-earnings-expected-move), [Maverick](https://www.mavericktrading.com/free-trading-videos/articles/implied-vs-actual-earnings-moves-is-the-market-overpricing-volatility), but this flips in high-realized-vol seasons [ORATS 2026](https://orats.com/blog/earnings-straddles-strong-season-2026). Use it as the market's forecast of "how big is a surprise," not as a sell-vol edge. **P**
3. **Thesis.** State it as a conditional: "If X prints > consensus + 1σ, then Y because Z, and the market will under/over-react because of W." Classify the news type (quantitative-fundamental → drift; qualitative/anticipated → fade).
4. **Instrument.** Single stock (idiosyncratic risk, max payoff), sector ETF (keeps the theme, drops single-name risk — tariff-day sector moves ranged 4% to 17%, so the sector choice mattered more than the index [SF Fed](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2025/10/market-reactions-to-tariff-announcements/)), index (macro), or pair (isolates the relative effect). Options only when defined risk is worth paying rich event IV.
5. **Entry timing.** *Do not trade the print.* A 300 ms delay cut news-strategy returns by 3.08% across all announcements, more on high-impact days [Scholtus, van Dijk & Frijns 2014](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v38y2014icp89-105.html); earnings "almost always" cause instantaneous jumps and post-2016 post-announcement trading looks efficiently priced [Christensen et al., JFE 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08962); most earnings price discovery happens after-hours [Jiang, Likitapiwat & McInish 2012](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/information-content-of-earnings-announcements-evidence-from-afterhours-trading/39171BF13B8D5EE4890B930F2FF4E19C). The "wait 15–30 minutes" rule is folklore with an academic cousin: the first half-hour S&P return predicts the last half-hour, especially on macro-news days [Gao, Han, Li & Zhou 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X18301351). Practical version: enter after the opening range is set, in the direction of the first 30 minutes, only if the thesis still holds. **P**
6. **Invalidation.** A price level (beyond the event-day extreme, or 1.5–2× ATR) *and* a thesis condition (guidance walked back, deal challenged, supply restored). If either triggers, exit.
7. **Position size.** From the distance to invalidation, not conviction (Section 6).
8. **Holding horizon.** Drift trades: 5–60 trading days (PEAD 60-day window; post-FOMC 15 days). Fade trades: 1–10 days (geopolitical recovery ~28 days average; qualitative news gives back over weeks).
9. **Exit rules.** Target at a pre-set R-multiple or on thesis completion; time stop if nothing has happened by the horizon; trail with ATR once >1R in profit.

Why the second-order trade is the feasible one: the first-order reaction is a speed contest retail loses by construction; second-order effects (drift as slow money re-prices, sector rotation, repair-or-not in supply shocks) unfold over days, where speed is irrelevant and the documented anomalies actually live.

## 4. Technical analysis: what has evidence vs. what does not

| Tool | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Time-series momentum / trend-following** | Strongest TA evidence | 58 futures/forwards, 12-month lookback, persists 1–12 months then partially reverses; best in extreme markets [Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X11002613); positive in every decade since 1880 [Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2993026). **A** |
| **Long-horizon moving averages (10-month / 200-day)** | Good as *risk filter*, weak as alpha | Faber's 10-month SMA: equity-like returns with bond-like volatility and drawdown [Faber 2007](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=962461). BLL 1992 found 26 MA/breakout rules beat cash on the Dow 1897–1986 [BLL](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1992.tb04681.x), but the best rule failed in the next 10 years out-of-sample once data-snooping was controlled [Sullivan, Timmermann & White 1999](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/0022-1082.00163). **A for drawdown control, B for returns** |
| **Short MAs / crossovers, RSI, MACD** | No standalone edge after costs | Survey of 95 modern studies: 56 positive, 20 negative, 19 mixed, with profits concentrated in FX/futures and eroded by costs and snooping [Park & Irwin 2007](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-6419.2007.00519.x); RSI/MACD profits vanish after transaction costs, and RSI failed even pre-cost in some samples [MPRA](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/54149/1/MPRA_paper_54149.pdf), [India study](https://mgmt.cmb.ac.lk/cbj/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/2.-Technical-trading-rules.pdf). **B/F** |
| **Volatility scaling / vol targeting** | Well supported as risk tool | Cutting exposure when vol is high raises Sharpe and alpha [Moreira & Muir 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12513); Sharpe gain holds for equities/credit only, but left-tail reduction holds across all asset classes [Harvey et al. 2018](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3175538). **A** |
| **ATR stops / stop-loss rules** | Supported *conditionally* | Under a random walk stops reduce expected return; under momentum they add value (50–100 bps/month during stop-out periods, 1950–2004) [Kaminski & Lo 2014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S138641811300030X). ATR/RSI/ADX all originate in [Wilder 1978](https://archive.org/details/newconceptsintec00wild). **A (as risk tool)** |
| **52-week high proximity** | Supported | Nearness to the 52-week high dominates past returns as a momentum signal and does not reverse long-run [George & Hwang 2004](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2004.00695.x). **A** |
| **Volume confirmation** | Supported as conditioner | Past volume separates continuation from reversal [Lee & Swaminathan 2000]; abnormal volume flags attention-driven retail buying [Barber & Odean 2008](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/21/2/785/1607197). **A/B** |
| **Support/resistance, round numbers** | Mixed; intraday FX only | Published S/R levels predicted intraday trend interruptions in FX, varying by firm and pair [Osler 2000](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf). **B** |
| **Opening-range breakout / day-trading systems** | Not established | Zarattini & Aziz claim 33% alpha (TQQQ +1,484%, 2016–23) [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4416622); replication finds break-even at ~2.2¢/share slippage with 76% of PnL from 2022 alone [replication](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq); a 2010–2026 re-run shows Sharpe ≈ −0.06 [danfin](https://danfin.net/opening-range-breakout-research). **F/B** |
| **Gaps / overnight vs intraday** | Structural, not a "gap fill" rule | Returns continue within the overnight or intraday period and reverse across them; momentum profits accrue overnight [Lou, Polk & Skouras 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v134y2019i1p192-213.html). "Gaps always fill" is folklore. **B / F** |

**Minimal defensible toolkit for an AI agent** (none is a standalone signal):

1. **Trend state**: 20/50/200-day SMA alignment and price vs 200-day — a regime filter (long drift trades only above the 200-day, or size down below it).
2. **14-day RSI** — over-extension flag for fades (>70/<30 with attention/volume), never a standalone entry.
3. **14-day ATR** — stop distance and position sizing unit (Wilder; Kaminski-Lo).
4. **20-day realized volatility** — vol-scaled sizing and a "halt" trigger (Moreira-Muir; Harvey).
5. **Distance from 52-week high/low** (George-Hwang).
6. **5-day and 20-day returns** — short-term reversal risk (5d) and intermediate continuation (20d).
7. **Relative strength vs SPY (20/60-day)** — cross-sectional momentum (Jegadeesh-Titman).
8. **Volume ÷ 20-day average** — confirmation for drift, attention warning for fades.
9. **Gap %** — size of the initial reaction, compared with the implied move.
10. Add **implied move** and **surprise ÷ consensus dispersion** as the two event-specific fields.

## 5. Retail reality

- Households at a large US discount broker (1991–96) turned over 75% of their portfolios annually; the most active quintile earned 11.4% vs 17.9% for the market, ~3.7% underperformance driven by costs and overconfidence [Barber & Odean 2000](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0022-1082.00226).
- Taiwan day traders (1992–2006, ~360,000 traders): >80% lose money; <1% predictably earn positive abnormal returns net of fees; the top 500 earned 61 bps/day gross but 38 bps net — skill exists but is rare and costs eat half of it [Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean 2014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418113000190).
- Brazilian equity-futures day traders (all who started 2013–15): of those persisting >300 days, **97% lost money**, ~1% earned more than minimum wage, 0.4–0.5% earned more than a bank teller ($54/day), and the *best* individual made $310/day with a $2,560 daily standard deviation; no evidence of learning with experience [Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti](https://ideas.repec.org/p/spa/wpaper/2019wpecon47.html), [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101).
- EU regulators found 74–89% of retail CFD accounts lose money, average losses €1,600–€29,000 per client [ESMA 2018](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/library/esma71-98-128_press_release_product_intervention.pdf).
- Behavioral mechanics: investors are 1.5–2× likelier to sell winners than losers, and the held losers underperform [Odean 1998](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0022-1082.00072); herding into top movers [Barber et al. 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.13183); at prop firms the commonest failure is breaching the daily-loss cap via oversized or revenge trades, not missing the profit target [FTMO rules summary](https://www.edgeflo.com/blog/ftmo-rules).

What the surviving minority share, per the same literature and practitioner consensus: skill that *persists* (the Taiwan top decile stayed profitable), low turnover relative to peers, defined risk per trade, positive expectancy measured in R-multiples over large samples [Tharp via TraderLion](https://traderlion.com/risk-management/r-and-r-multiples/), few high-conviction trades rather than chasing headlines (the attention literature is a catalogue of what *not* to buy), and a journal — the Brazilian "no learning" result is about unstructured experience; structured feedback is the only plausible route to the 1%.

**Design implication:** build the system first as a **learning + paper-trading environment** with mandatory per-trade fields (catalyst type, thesis, priced-in estimate, invalidation, R at risk, horizon) and an expectancy dashboard (win rate, average R win/loss, expectancy, max drawdown, trades/week). Graduate a template to real money only after ≥50 paper trades with positive expectancy and drawdown inside the limits below — the only way to measure the 97% base rate against yourself before it costs anything.

## 6. Risk and sizing rules worth encoding

- **Fixed-fractional risk.** Standard: 1–2% of equity per trade, risk = entry-to-stop distance × size [Elder's 2% rule](https://tickertape.tdameritrade.com/trading/rules-to-manage-risk-15908). **Aggressive variant:** 3–5% per trade *only* with (a) a hard daily loss cap of 5–6% (prop-firm standard: 5% daily / 10% total) [FTMO](https://ftmo.com/en/trading-objectives/), (b) a monthly circuit breaker at 6–10% (Elder's 6% rule: no new trades once realized + open risk hits 6%), and (c) max 2–3 concurrent positions so heat stays ≤10–15%.
- **Kelly and fractional Kelly.** Kelly maximizes log-wealth growth but routinely produces >50% drawdowns; half-Kelly captures ~3/4 of the growth rate at roughly half the variance [MacLean, Ziemba & Blazenko via Kelly/Ziemba chapter](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/mckinnon/blackouts/StochOptFinanceAndEnergySpringer/Chap1_KellyZiemba.pdf); small errors in estimated edge cause massive over-betting [MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba 2011](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2011.619561). Rule: compute Kelly from the template's *paper* expectancy, bet ≤ quarter-Kelly, capped by the fixed-fractional limit.
- **ATR stops and R-multiples.** Stop = 1.5–2.5 × 14-day ATR beyond invalidation; 1R = that distance × shares. Log every trade in R; expectancy = mean R [Tharp](https://traderlion.com/risk-management/r-and-r-multiples/). Stops add value in trending regimes and subtract in random-walk regimes [Kaminski & Lo](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S138641811300030X) — so pair them with the trend filter.
- **Volatility scaling.** Target position vol = (target risk) ÷ (20-day realized vol); cut size when vol spikes [Moreira & Muir](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12513), [Harvey et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3175538).
- **Max drawdown halts.** Flat for the day at −5% (aggressive) / −3% (standard); flat for the week at −8%; halve all sizes after a 10% peak-to-trough drawdown until a new high. Momentum books also de-risk after a vol spike following a market decline — when momentum crashes occur [Daniel & Moskowitz](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439).
- **Correlation / portfolio heat.** Sum of open R ("heat") ≤ 6% standard, ≤ 12–15% aggressive [portfolio heat](https://journalplus.co/learn/glossary/portfolio-heat/); count same-sector or same-macro-driver positions as one (tariff-day sector moves were all in the *same* direction).
- **No averaging down.** The disposition effect is the most robust retail pathology [Odean 1998](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0022-1082.00072); adding below invalidation is its mechanical form. Add only to winners above 1R with the stop at breakeven.
- **Time stops for catalyst trades.** Drift templates: exit if <+0.5R after 10 trading days (PEAD is front-loaded; post-FOMC drift is a 15-day effect). Fade templates: exit if the reversal has not started within 3 sessions (over-reactions unwind over weeks, but the *start* is fast).

## 7. Playbook: reusable trade templates

| # | Template | Trigger | Instrument / direction | Horizon | Invalidation | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Earnings drift after big beat + raised guidance** | SUE in top decile *and* guidance raised; price gaps up but closes inside or above the implied move; above 200-day SMA | Long the stock (or long stock / short sector ETF pair) entered after the first 30 min or on day-2 pullback | 10–60 days | Close below the event-day low, or guidance walked back | **Strong** (PEAD; guidance dominance; quant-news underreaction) |
| 2 | **Earnings miss + cut guidance drift** | Bottom-decile SUE with lowered guidance; below 200-day | Short stock / long sector ETF, or long puts | 10–40 days | Close above event-day high | **Strong** (mirror of PEAD; downward guidance penalty is larger) |
| 3 | **Fade the first spike on ambiguous macro** | CPI/NFP within ±1σ of consensus, yet the index moves >1× its typical event move in 5 min with no confirmation from rates | SPY/QQQ against the spike, entered after 15–30 min once the first half-hour direction stalls | Intraday to 2 days | New extreme beyond the spike | **Weak** (case evidence; Gao et al. shows first-half-hour *continuation*, so only take it once the first half-hour has itself reversed) |
| 4 | **Hawkish surprise → long USD / short duration** | Hawkish target or path surprise (fed-funds futures reprice up; statement/SEP more hawkish than consensus) | Long UUP/DXY; short TLT or long TBT; short-bias equities | 5–15 days | Fed-funds futures retrace the surprise, or the next data print reverses the path | **Medium** (GSS target/path; Faust et al. dollar response; Neuhierl-Weber equity drift) |
| 5 | **Dovish surprise → long equities 15 days** | Expansionary policy surprise | Long SPY/QQQ, larger in high-uncertainty regimes | 15 days | Surprise reversed by subsequent Fed communication | **Medium** (Neuhierl-Weber; untested post-publication) |
| 6 | **Sector ETF on regulatory shock** | Rule/ruling that changes cash flows for an industry; sector-vs-index dispersion >5% on day 1 | Short the sector ETF (or long the beneficiary sector), not single names; if the policy is reversible, flip to fade after the second headline | 5–30 days | Policy paused/reversed, or sector recovers half the gap | **Medium** (cases: China ed drift; tariff reversal) |
| 7 | **Merger-arb spread on a friendly cash deal** | Cash deal, spread 3–7%, low antitrust risk, financing committed | Long target (no hedge needed for cash deals) | To close (3–9 months) | Regulatory challenge, financing failure, spread widens >2× | **Strong** pattern, **thin** payoff (+2.0% / −2.8%) |
| 8 | **Geopolitical shock fade** | Military/terror event without lasting supply impairment; S&P down 3–8% | Scale into the index on the second or third down day; long oil only if supply is *actually* impaired | 10–40 days | Escalation with real supply or financial-system impact; new low after 5 days | **Medium** (19/20 events recovered in ~28 days) |
| 9 | **Oil supply shock → XLE/USO, then fade** | Physical outage >2% of world supply | Long USO/XLE on the gap only while repair is uncertain; fade the spike on the first repair headline | 2–20 days | Repair timeline announced (fade) / repair delayed (hold) | **Medium** (EIA/CRS case; OPEC event study) |
| 10 | **Crypto ETF-flow momentum** | 5-day net spot-ETF inflow streak >$1B with rising BTC; no macro risk-off | Long BTC/IBIT (MSTR/COIN as high-beta) | 2–10 days | Net outflow day >$500M, or BTC below 20-day SMA | **Weak/medium** (same-day impact strong, but shocks reverse; edge depends on flow persistence) |
| 11 | **Crypto sell-the-news** | Long-anticipated positive event (ETF approval, upgrade, halving) after a >30% run-up | Reduce/short into the event, cover 1–2 weeks after | 3–15 days | New high within 3 days of the event | **Folklore with cases** (ETF approval −15%; Merge −9%/−20%; tiny n) |
| 12 | **Exchange failure vs. hack** | Hack with solvent exchange → fade the dip in 24–72h; solvency/fraud → short or stand aside until contagion clears | BTC/ETH, COIN | 1–10 days | Hack: withdrawals halted (reclassify as solvency). Solvency: on-chain outflows stop | **Practitioner** (FTX −22%; Bybit −2–4%) |

**Usage rules.** Every template runs paper-only until it has ≥50 logged trades with positive expectancy in R. Templates 1, 2 and 7 have the strongest priors; 3, 11 and 12 are the likeliest hindsight fictions and get the smallest size. Apply the McLean-Pontiff haircut: assume half the paper return, smaller in mega-caps and larger in less liquid names.
